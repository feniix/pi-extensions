 # Laya vs Jev for typed decisions

 ## Bottom line

 Jev is the better starting hypothesis for varied, zero-shot semantic decisions. Laya is worth evaluating for local/private inference, small fixed decision
 spaces, or a domain where you can fine-tune and calibrate a specialist. This is a recommendation from published evidence, not a benchmark run in this
 repository.

 Both return Choice (closed-set selection), Score (ordinal judgment), and Noul (probability of yes), rather than generated prose. Neither replaces
 deterministic authorization or a general reasoning model. Sources: [Laya
 README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ccjjfdyqlhy/laya/refs/heads/main/README.md), [Jev Choice](https://docs.typesafe.ai/primitives/choice.md), [Jev
 limitations](https://docs.typesafe.ai/model-jaggedness/jev-1.13.md).

 ## Practical differences

 ### The important benchmark catch

 Laya’s specialist reports 76.6% accuracy versus Jev’s published 72.7%, but:
 - Laya was fine-tuned on that benchmark’s training split.
 - Untuned Laya scored only 34–36%.
 - Jev had better probability-distribution matching and lower calibration error.
 - The publisher explicitly says this was not a controlled head-to-head.

 So “Laya beats Jev” is too broad. The evidence supports “a tuned Laya specialist can outperform published Jev results on its target workflows.” Model card

 ### My recommendation

 For decisions such as source relevance, evidence sufficiency, or routing, I’d start with Jev, then benchmark Laya if local processing or customization
 matters.

 For spending approval, permissions, accounting, or retry authorization, use neither as the authority: keep those rules deterministic. Confidence summarizes
 a model’s distribution—not permission or guaranteed correctness. Jev confidence
